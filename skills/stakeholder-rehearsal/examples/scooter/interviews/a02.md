Q1: Did you notice the Courier's post about councillors questioning whether the 40 km of lanes is funded? Why did you or didn't you react to it?
A1: I didn't just notice it—I broke that story. We got word from councillors that the 40 km is only "promised," not funded, and that was too important to sit on. We posted it because it proved what we'd been saying: the city announced a ban but hasn't done the groundwork. We kept hitting the immediate deadline—fines on June 1 with zero lanes now—because that's where the pressure is. The councillors' comment was validation, not the main point.

Q2: What would make you call a protest?
A2: If Transport Office doesn't meet with us by tomorrow morning, we're organizing one. We've been asking all day—we said it politely at first, then direct, then we made it clear we need specifics and a meeting date. By end of day Day 1, they haven't said a word to us, and Quick-Bite is still dancing around specifics. If we're ignored for another day, riders are going to see that the city and the platforms don't care what happens to them on June 1, and we'll show that our members matter.
